Neighborhood Retrofit Spillovers

When one household insulates its walls or installs a heat pump, the neighbors notice. This lab simulates 400 households on a street grid, gives a quarter of them a retrofit voucher, and asks what a standard regression misses when spending decisions spill across the fence.

Rahat Sabyrbekov, PhD · environmental & applied microeconomics · github.com/rsabyrbekov

From household choice to a spatial autoregressive model

Preferences

Household i picks retrofit spending yi. Its private return depends on income, tenure, building age and the voucher. A complementarity term rewards matching what neighbors spend (shared contractors, information, social norms).

Ui = aiyi − ½yi2 + ρ yi Σj wijyj
Best response and equilibrium

The first-order condition gives a linear best response. Stacking all households, the unique Nash equilibrium (for |ρ| < 1) is the SAR model.

y = ρWy + Xβ + ε ⇒ y = (I − ρW)−1(Xβ + ε)
What a policy does

A voucher shifts one household's ai, then ripples outward through rounds of best responses. Averaging the impact matrix gives direct, indirect and total effects (LeSage & Pace).

∂y/∂xk′ = βk(I − ρW)−1, total = βk/(1 − ρ)

W is rook contiguity on a 20 × 20 torus, row-standardized. Its eigenvalues have a closed form, so exact maximum likelihood, analytic standard errors and the impact decomposition all run in your browser. Data are simulated; amber numbers are the true values the estimator is trying to recover.
